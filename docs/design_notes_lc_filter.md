# Design Notes: Inverter Output LC and LCL Filter Design

This document details the mathematical design methodology for the output filters of the **48V Open-Source Microgrid Research Platform** (Single-Phase Voltage Source Inverter, $500\text{ W}$, $V_{dc} = 48\text{ V}$, $f_{sw} = 20\text{ kHz}$).

---

## 1. System Parameters

| Parameter | Symbol | Value | Units |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Nominal DC Bus Voltage | $V_{dc}$ | $48$ | $\text{V}$ |
| Nominal Output Power | $P_{nom}$ | $500$ | $\text{W}$ |
| Fundamental Grid/Output Frequency | $f_o$ | $50$ | $\text{Hz}$ |
| Switching Frequency | $f_{sw}$ | $20,000$ | $\text{Hz}$ |
| Modulation Index | $m_a$ | $0.85$ | - |
| Output AC Peak Voltage | $V_{ac,pk} = m_a \cdot V_{dc}$ | $40.8$ | $\text{V}$ |
| Output AC RMS Voltage | $V_{ac,rms} = \frac{V_{ac,pk}}{\sqrt{2}}$ | $\approx 28.85$ | $\text{V}$ |
| Nominal RMS Output Current | $I_{ac,rms} = \frac{P_{nom}}{V_{ac,rms}}$ | $\approx 17.33$ | $\text{A}$ |
| Peak Output Current | $I_{ac,pk} = \sqrt{2} I_{ac,rms}$ | $\approx 24.51$ | $\text{A}$ |
| Nominal Load Resistance | $R_{load} = \frac{V_{ac,rms}^2}{P_{nom}}$ | $\approx 1.66$ | $\Omega$ |

## 2. Stage 1: LC Filter Design (Stand-alone / Islanded Load)

### 2.1 Inductor Sizing ($L_f$)
The filter inductor limits the high-frequency current ripple generated by SPWM switching.
For unipolar SPWM, the effective switching frequency ripple is at $2 f_{sw} = 40\text{ kHz}$.
The maximum peak-to-peak inductor current ripple $\Delta I_{L,max}$ occurs at a duty cycle $D = 0.5$:

$$\Delta I_{L,max} = \frac{V_{dc}}{4 \cdot f_{sw} \cdot L_f} \quad \text{(Bipolar SPWM)}$$
$$\Delta I_{L,max} = \frac{V_{dc}}{8 \cdot f_{sw} \cdot L_f} \quad \text{(Unipolar SPWM)}$$

Targeting a current ripple ratio of $r = \frac{\Delta I_{L,max}}{I_{ac,pk}} \approx 20\% - 30\%$:
$$\Delta I_{L,target} = 0.25 \times 24.51\text{ A} \approx 6.13\text{ A}$$

For Unipolar SPWM:
$$L_f \ge \frac{V_{dc}}{8 \cdot f_{sw} \cdot \Delta I_{L,target}} = \frac{48}{8 \cdot 20000 \cdot 6.13} = \frac{48}{980,800} \approx 48.9\,\mu\text{H}$$

To provide extra margin against core saturation and reduce ripple under low-power conditions, we select:
$$L_f = 100\,\mu\text{H}$$
With $L_f = 100\,\mu\text{H}$, $\Delta I_{L,max} = \frac{48}{8 \cdot 20000 \cdot 100 \times 10^{-6}} = 3.0\text{ A}$ ($12.2\%$ of peak current).

### 2.2 Capacitor Sizing ($C_f$)
The filter capacitor absorbs the high-frequency current harmonics and sets the low-pass cutoff frequency.
Two constraints dictate the capacitor sizing:
1. **Reactive power limitation**: At fundamental frequency, the reactive power drawn by $C_f$ should not exceed $5\%$ of rated power:
   $$Q_C = \omega_o C_f V_{ac,rms}^2 \le 0.05 \cdot P_{nom}$$
   $$C_f \le \frac{0.05 \cdot 500}{2 \pi \cdot 50 \cdot (28.85)^2} = \frac{25}{314.16 \cdot 832.3} \approx 95.6\,\mu\text{F}$$

2. **Cutoff Frequency Requirement**:
   The cutoff frequency $f_c$ of the LC filter must satisfy:
   $$10 f_o < f_c < 0.2 f_{sw}$$
   $$500\text{ Hz} < f_c < 4,000\text{ Hz}$$
   The natural frequency is:
   $$f_c = \frac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{L_f C_f}}$$

Selecting $C_f = 47\,\mu\text{F}$:
$$f_c = \frac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{100 \times 10^{-6} \cdot 47 \times 10^{-6}}} = \frac{1}{2\pi \cdot 6.855 \times 10^{-5}} \approx 2,321\text{ Hz}$$
This comfortably sits within the desired range ($500\text{ Hz} < 2,321\text{ Hz} < 4,000\text{ Hz}$).

### 2.3 Transfer Function & Damping
The open-loop transfer function with load resistance $R_{load}$ is:
$$G_{LC}(s) = \frac{V_o(s)}{V_{inv}(s)} = \frac{1}{L_f C_f s^2 + \frac{L_f}{R_{load}} s + 1}$$

Under light load ($R_{load} \rightarrow \infty$), damping ratio $\zeta \rightarrow 0$.
To prevent peaking around $f_c$, passive damping via a series damping resistor $R_d$ with $C_f$ can be applied:
$$R_d \approx \frac{1}{3} \frac{1}{2\pi f_c C_f} = \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{2\pi \cdot 2321 \cdot 47 \times 10^{-6}} \approx 0.48\,\Omega$$

## 3. Stage 2: LCL Filter Design Preview (Grid Connection)

When connecting directly to the utility grid or an AC bus, an **LCL filter** provides $-60\text{ dB/decade}$ attenuation above its resonant frequency, drastically reducing required inductor physical size and losses compared to an LC filter:

- Inverter-side Inductor: $L_1$ (attenuates current ripple from bridge)
- Filter Capacitor: $C_f$ (provides low-impedance path for high frequencies)
- Grid-side Inductor: $L_2$ (attenuates harmonics into grid, provides decoupling)
- Damping Resistor: $R_d$ (damps resonant peak without active control complications)

Resonant frequency:
$$f_{res} = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{L_1 + L_2}{L_1 L_2 C_f}}$$
